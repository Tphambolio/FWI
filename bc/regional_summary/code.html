Fire Weather Index — BC Danger Rating

BC Wildfire Service uses 5 classes — no "Very High"; adds "Very Low". The left half of each map marker shows the danger class.

Class FWI Range Meaning
Very Low0 – 4Fuels wet, spread very unlikely
Low5 – 11Fuels moist, spread unlikely
Moderate12 – 20Some fire activity possible
High21 – 33Rapid spread, control difficult
Extreme34+Crown fire conditions, evacuate
Head Fire Intensity — Byram Class

HFI (kW/m) is the energy output at the fire front from FBP. The right half of each marker shows the intensity class. Click any marker for the kW/m value.

Class HFI (kW/m) Meaning
1-Low< 10Direct attack with hand tools · Should anchor
2-Mod10 – 499Direct attack with hand tools · Should anchor
3-High500 – 1 999Direct attack with pump and hose · Should anchor
4-VH2 000 – 3 999Indirect attack · Direct on less intense area · Must anchor
5-Ext4 000 – 9 999Indirect attack · Direct on less intense area · Must anchor

BC fire weather stations grouped by Fire Centre. Source says where each row's FWI comes from, in plain language: OBSERVED (station sensor or today's agency chain), CARRIED (yesterday's chain stepped forward one day) or MODEL (Open-Meteo forecast weather), with its age — amber over 3 h, red over 24 h — and the network (CWFIS · BCWS · MSC SWOB · Open-Meteo) underneath. Hover or long-press a chip for the technical detail. FWI is the daily value at noon LST. HFI CLS is Byram intensity class using C3 lodgepole pine fuel.
